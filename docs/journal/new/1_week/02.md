## Shape decisions
- Shape B: Portfolio is a real entity; PortfolioHolding is the join table.
- `ForeignKey("table.col")` strings throughout, not class attributes.
- `Mapped[T]` for type, `mapped_column(...)` for schema rule.
- `Text` (not VARCHAR) for free-form LLM text; `String(N)` only when N is a real contract.
- `server_default=func.now()` on every `*_at` column; never Python-side `default=datetime.utcnow`.
- No `relationship()` helpers yet - deferred to YAGNI; FK joins only.
- No `embedding` column - pgvector extension + dim decision deferred (out of #03 scope).
- No `unique=True` on any FK - 1:1 enforcement deferred.

## What I think I learned (to be revised in full reflection)
- `Mapped[X]` = annotation (door). `mapped_column(...)` = schema rule (furnished room).
- Float is right for visualization math; Numeric is right for money math. The choice follows the kind of math that runs on the column.

## Types I still get tripped on (to be expanded)
- The "no fix" ForeignKey answer (string vs class attribute)
- I picked one but didn't defend it from first principles.
- `Mapped[str | None]` vs `Mapped[str]` with `nullable=True` at the column-only level - when I see one I have to consciously translate to the other.

## What I don't understand yet
- SQLAlchemy 2.x is permissive by default: `Mapped[str] = mapped_column(Integer)` does NOT raise at runtime. Only mypy enforces the type. Why is the contract so loose, and what's the production-grade way to enforce it (custom `__init_subclass__`? mypy plugin in CI? something else)?
- Composite FK indexes (e.g., `(portfolio_id, company_id)`) vs two single-column indexes: I said "two are enough" — but I don't really know when Postgres switches from index-scan to bitmap-merge, or what triggers that decision.
